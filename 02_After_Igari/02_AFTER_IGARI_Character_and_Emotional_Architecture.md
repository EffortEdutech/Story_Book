# AFTER IGARI - Character and Emotional Architecture

## Adam Idris

Adam is the sole deep third-person point of view.

He is a Malaysian Muslim husband and father travelling as an ordinary passenger. His defining flaw is not lack of love, but the habit of delaying presence. He believes responsibility can be proven through work, provision, competence, and eventual return.

Adam's arc moves from control to surrender, from deferred tenderness to present attention, and from fixing as love to presence as love.

## Nadia

Nadia must remain fully human, not merely a corrective force for Adam.

She understands the emotional cost of Adam's absences, but she is not written as a scold or symbol. Her marriage to Adam contains love, fatigue, disappointment, tenderness, and history.

Her role is to hold the domestic truth Adam avoids: love cannot always be deferred until work is finished.

## Hana

Hana is eleven.

She must remain a believable child: intelligent, emotionally perceptive, curious, sometimes direct, never written as a mystical device. Her voice message is the manuscript's emotional pressure point.

Key elements:

- she asks about stars;
- Adam promises, "I'll tell you tomorrow";
- the Sunday/Saturn promise must be withheld until its locked moment;
- her message remains unopened through Part I.

## Rahman

Rahman is Adam's father.

He represents quiet provision and ordinary amanah. His lessons should be restrained, lived, and specific rather than sermon-like.

Part I uses Rahman to strengthen Adam's belief that responsibility means provision. Later material related to Rahman's illness and Adam's guilt must remain withheld until the architecture allows it.

## Salmah

Salmah is Adam's mother.

She enters through sensory memory, domestic texture, and Johor-rooted family feeling. She should feel natural and non-symbolic.

## Emotional Progression

Adam must remain recognisable and sympathetic even when wrong.

He should not become too self-aware too early. His transformation must be gradual. The manuscript should show behaviour before explanation and use restraint rather than confession-heavy exposition.

## Relationship Rules

- Adam loves Nadia and Hana.
- His failure is delay, not indifference.
- Nadia's pain is valid without making Adam villainous.
- Hana's innocence must never become manipulative sentimentality.
- Rahman and Salmah must remain ordinary parents, not symbolic machinery.
