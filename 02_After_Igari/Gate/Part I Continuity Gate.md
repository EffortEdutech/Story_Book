## Corrections required before Chapter 10

C1

### Chapter 6 ends asleep, Chapter 7 opens at the laptop

prose only

Chapter 6 ends with the laptop under the seat, the tray put away, the phone back in the pocket, and Adam closing his eyes on the Johor rain. Chapter 7 opens with him waking in front of the laptop on the tray table, after "three emails saved for later". The two cannot both be true.

**Fix:** rewrite only the first paragraphs of Chapter 7. Say that sleep did not come, that he took the laptop out after all, and that he has just closed it again. The Chapter 6 ending stays untouched. In Chapter 6, lowered the tray table should also read "raised" or "folded away".

C2

### Chapter 8 clock: too much happens inside one minute

timing lines only

Chapter 8 reads 01:19 when Adam wakes and stays at 01:19 through two memories, an argument with himself, a breathing count, a crew member passing and a near-sleep. The closing line says The minute display had not changed. That is the residue of the earlier 01:22 correction, which flattened the times without making room for the content. Also, Chapter 7 ends at 01:12, so Twelve minutes, perhaps of sleep cannot be right; the gap is at most seven.

**Fix:** keep the header at 01:19 and treat it as the closing anchor, the way Chapter 5's header is the takeoff moment. Make the opening check about 01:14, the middle check about 01:15, and the last check 01:19 just before "Then Adam slept". Change "Twelve minutes" to "Two minutes, perhaps". Chapter 9's "barely a minute since he had last been awake" stays true, and the chapter ends on the minute of the final radio call without Adam knowing it.

C3

### Chapter 9 clock: your decision needed

touches a frozen note

Chapter 9 holds the watch at 01:20 from start to finish (Still 01:20, lowered his hand before the minute could change). In that time a crew member walks the aisle pouring water, Adam runs two breathing counts, talks himself out of the phone twice and watches stars dim. That is several minutes of events. The ledger says "Chapter 9 uses approximately 01:20", and Chapter 10 starts at 01:28, so there is room.

**Options:** (A, recommended) open at 01:20 and let later checks read about 01:22, 01:24 and 01:26; four timing lines change. The sensations then fall after the official IGARI passage, which is closer to the record. (B) keep every check at 01:20 and cut roughly a third of the chapter's events. Because this reads against a frozen note, I have not assumed either.

C4

### Cues that imply a technical or turning event

prose only

The rules say no unsupported technical explanation and no objective turn asserted. The cues are hedged, but together they tell a story of something happening to the aircraft.

- Chapter 9: Even if there had been a change in engine tone. Had there? and the Steady. Then not steady. Then steady again. passage. Nothing in the record supports a passenger-audible engine change. Cut both and keep the "attention makes repetition uneven" explanation.
- Chapter 9: leaning, pressure "from shoulder to hip", the window frame no longer level, and a star disappearing. Four directional sensations in one minute read as a banking turn. Keep two, make them non-directional (weight, pressure), and drop the window-frame and star beats.
- Chapter 8: The stars had shifted relative to the frame. This sits before the final radio call, when the aircraft was still on its filed route. Remove it or replace it with a neutral observation.

C5

### POV breaches and authorial winks

prose only

- **Chapter 1, last lines:** Behind him, his cold coffee remained beneath the seat until a cleaner noticed it several minutes later. Adam cannot see this. Chapter 2 already lets him infer it, so cut the Chapter 1 sentence. Then In Adam's pocket, Hana's message stayed unopened. For now. is an omniscient wink; end on the unopened message without "For now".
- **Chapter 5:** The pilots knew. Somebody somewhere had told them where to go. That was enough. Reads as heavy irony about the cockpit and route. Keep "The aircraft knew" at most, or cut the three lines.
- **Chapter 8:** Not disappearing for a month. The word lands as a wink in this novel. Use "gone for a month". Chapter 9 has the same problem in the world could disappear completely.
- **Chapter 6:** The last thing he thought of was not Beijing. "Last thing" is the banned "last time" register. Use "The thing he thought of as he drifted".
- **Chapter 5:** For one brief moment Adam could never quite describe implies a retrospective narrator. Use "could not have described".

C6

### Two historical figures to source-check

H1

- **IGARI time.** The ledger locks 01:20:31. The Malaysian preliminary report (as reproduced by [The Borneo Post](https://www.theborneopost.com/2014/05/02/mh-370-preliminary-report/)) gives 01:21:04 for IGARI and 01:21:13 for the last radar return. The sources differ, so confirm against the final Safety Investigation Report and record one citation in the ledger. Chapter 9's "approximately 01:20" survives either way, because the text never states the time. Takeoff (00:41:43, Runway 32R, rounded to 00:42) and the final call (acknowledged just after 01:19:24) check out.
- **Chapter 5 opening:** The aircraft began to move at 00:31. I could not verify a pushback or taxi time in that report. Remove the clock stamp ("The aircraft began to move."), and fix Four minutes had passed at 00:36, which is five minutes after 00:31.

## Recommended, not blocking

- **Adam's self-awareness runs slightly ahead of plan.** The narrator labels his psychology: The justification settled comfortably (Ch 4), The explanation pleased him more than it should have and Adam recognised it this time (Ch 5), The defence rose quickly because it had been rehearsed before and where explanations went (Ch 8), Adam noticed it this time (Ch 9). The behaviour already shows the pattern. Cutting these labels keeps "behaviour before explanation" and leaves Part II room to let him notice.
- **The same beat repeats in Chapters 3 to 9:** phone out, thumb hovers, interruption or rationalisation, phone away. It is deliberate, but seven in a row is heavy. Thin one beat each in Chapters 3, 4 and 5, and vary the interruption.
- **Seam, Chapters 2 to 3:** Chapter 2 ends at about 23:33 with the line moving "steadily"; Chapter 3 shows 23:47 as he enters the walkway. One line in Chapter 3 about the bridge queue stalling closes the gap, since the 23:47 header is frozen.

- **Seatmate tic:** "Same." appears three times across Chapters 3 and 4, and the "Work?" exchange repeats. Trim one.
- **2014 messaging:** the "three dots" (Ch 3, Farid) are iMessage-style; WhatsApp then showed "typing…". Two grey ticks are correct. Never use blue ticks later (introduced late 2014).
- **Smaller items:** "One night away" (Ch 3) against a same-day return; "the man beside him's paperback" (Ch 5, grammar); Chapter 7's "finally, he stopped checking the time" pre-empts the Chapter 35 payoff; Chapter 7 uses "Rahman" where Adam would think "Abah"; the phone battery drops from 81% to 76% in about 17 minutes in flight mode.
- **Earphones:** they are in the roller bag, which goes in the overhead bin in Chapter 3, and the thread is dropped. Chapter 27 needs a decision: speaker, or retrieved earphones.
- **Real-person check:** I found no passenger-specific details, and the seatmate and gate passengers are generic. I cannot check against the manifest, so a one-time comparison of Adam's profile (age 41, occupation, family) with the public passenger list is worth doing before publication.
